Kothapalli Waterfalls: Complete Visitor Guide, Timings, Route & Travel Tips

Araku Trails Desk
October 16, 2026
6 min read

Hidden in the serene tribal landscape of Gangaraju Madugula mandal near Paderu and Lambasingi, Kothapalli Waterfalls (often spelled Kothapally) has emerged as one of the most picturesque natural attractions in the Eastern Ghats of Andhra Pradesh.

Unlike vertical drops where water plunges straight into a deep pool, Kothapalli glides over a wide expanse of stepped rock terraces. This creates a gentle, roaring cascade that allows visitors to safely dip their feet and admire the misty river mist filtering through tall sal trees.

Quick Facts: Timings, Fees & Distance

Kothapalli is easily combinable with a 2-day or 3-day itinerary covering Araku Valley, Paderu, and Lambasingi.

Kothapalli Waterfalls At A Glance

Location: G. Madugula Mandal, Alluri Sitharama Raju (ASR) District, Andhra Pradesh.

From Paderu ~30 km
From Lambasingi ~40 km
From Araku ~65 km
From Vizag ~135 km

Timings & Entry Permits

  • Opening Hours: 6:00 AM to 5:30 PM (Daily)
  • Entry Fee: Approximately ₹10 – ₹50 per person (managed by local community/ITDA for maintenance and cleanup)
  • Parking: Dedicated parking space available for cars and bikes near the entrance gate
  • Walk to the Falls: Only about 100 metres down well-constructed stone steps — suitable for children and seniors!

What to Expect at the Falls

Kothapalli Waterfalls was discovered relatively recently by local tribal youngsters and gained viral popularity through photography and video documentaries. The river cascades over several tiers of layered rock formation, resembling a natural staircase.

  • Gentle Water Flow: During post-monsoon and winter months, the water spreads evenly across the rock shelves rather than hitting in a violent torrent.
  • Easy Access: Unlike remote forest treks (such as Thonkota or Katiki which require steep hikes), Kothapalli features paved stone steps with handrails leading straight from the vehicle parking area down to the water.
  • Photography Heaven: Morning sunlight filters through the surrounding dense canopy, making it a dream spot for nature photographers and portrait shots.

How to Reach from Vizag, Araku & Lambasingi

From Paderu Town • ~30 km (~50 mins)

Paderu → G. Madugula → Kothapalli

This is the shortest and most convenient route. The road is scenic, rolling through tribal villages, coffee estates, and gentle ghat curves.

From Araku Valley • ~65 km (~2 hours)

Araku → Paderu Road → Kothapalli

If staying in Araku, take the Araku-Paderu scenic hill highway. This route lets you combine morning coffee plantation tours in Araku with an afternoon waterfall visit.

From Lambasingi • ~40 km (~1 hr 15 mins)

Lambasingi → Chintapalli → G. Madugula → Kothapalli

A very popular combination for tourists staying in Lambasingi for cold winter nights. You can drive to Kothapalli Waterfalls after enjoying morning mist and strawberry farms.

Famous Bamboo Chicken & Local Tribal Snacks

One of the biggest culinary treats near Kothapalli Waterfalls is authentic Bamboo Chicken (Bongu Chicken) prepared by local tribal vendors near the parking area:

Must-Try Local Delicacies

  • Bongu Chicken: Marinated country chicken stuffed into fresh green bamboo stalks and slow-roasted over burning wood coals. Absolutely oil-free, aromatic, and delicious!
  • Roasted Corn on the Cob: Sweet mountain corn rubbed with lime and red chili salt.
  • Wild Hill Honey: Bottled organic forest honey harvested by local tribal gatherers.

Safety & Photography Tips

Visitor Advisory

Beware of Slippery Rocks: Moss-covered rock surfaces near the cascades are extremely slick when wet. Wear shoes with deep rubber treads. If travelling with children, keep them within arms reach near water edges.

  • Carry Extra Clothes: You will almost certainly want to dip your feet or sit near the misty spray. Changing stalls are available near the entrance.
  • Best Visiting Months: July through February offers peak water volume. By late March and April, the water flow diminishes significantly.
  • Avoid Heavy Downpours: During severe monsoon storms, flash runoff can turn water currents treacherous. Always heed local warnings.
