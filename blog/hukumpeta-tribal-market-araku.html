Hukumpeta Weekly Tribal Market: Araku's Authentic Culture, Spices & Wild Honey

Araku Trails Desk
October 16, 2026
5 min read

If you want to experience the true soul of Araku Valley beyond manicured resorts and souvenir shops, there is no better place than an authentic weekly tribal market (locally known as a Santha or Shandy). Among the most vibrant in the Eastern Ghats is the Hukumpeta Tribal Market.

Documented by Telugu documentary channel THE 461 CULTURE / AIO Delights in their field video "Hukumpeta Tribal Market", this open-air gathering is where indigenous communities (including the Kondhs, Bagatas, and Valmikis) walk down from remote mountain hamlets to trade wild forest treasures, aromatic spices, and hand-woven crafts.

Top Authentic Things to Buy at Hukumpeta

Buying directly from tribal gatherers not only ensures 100% purity but directly supports indigenous families without middlemen:

Authentic Local Produce

  • Wild Rock-Bee Forest Honey (Pitta Thene): Raw, unpasteurized honey gathered from high cliff hives in the Eastern Ghats. Rich in pollen and antioxidants.
  • High-Curcumin Hill Turmeric (Konda Pasupu): Unlike commercial turmeric powders, indigenous tribal turmeric from these hills is intensely aromatic with high natural medicinal potency.
  • Traditional Millets: Finger millet (Ragi), foxtail millet (Korra), and little millet (Samalu) freshly harvested from hill terraced fields.
  • Forest Long Pepper (Pippallu) & Wild Spices: Hand-picked wild spices used in traditional Ayurveda and local cooking.
  • Hand-Woven Bamboo Baskets: Sturdy winnowing fans and bamboo storage baskets made by local artisans.

How to Reach from Araku & Paderu

Hukumpeta is nestled in the valleys between Araku town and Paderu. The drive offers smooth winding roads flanked by silver oak trees and pineapple gardens.

  • From Araku Town (~35 km / 50 mins): Head south via the Araku-Paderu highway. Turn at the Hukumpeta junction.
  • From Paderu (~22 km / 35 mins): Head north-east towards Hukumpeta. The road is well-paved with minimal traffic.

Responsible Visitor Etiquette

Respectful Tourism Tips

Always Ask Before Taking Photos: The tribal people of Araku are humble and polite. Always smile and ask for permission before pointing your camera or phone at women sellers or children.

Carry Hard Cash: Mobile data networks can be erratic, and most tribal sellers do not use digital UPI payment QR codes. Keep small-denomination cash notes (₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹100).

Fair Bargaining: Remember that tribal farmers carry their harvest miles on foot over mountain trails. Pay them fair prices — what is a small sum to an urban traveller is livelihood for a hill family.
