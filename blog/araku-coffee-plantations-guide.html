From Paris luxury cafes to boutique roasteries worldwide, Araku Coffee has earned an iconic international reputation. Grown at an elevation of 900 to 1,100 metres in the biodiverse soil of the Eastern Ghats, this 100% organic, shade-grown Arabica coffee is one of India's proudest agricultural exports.

Featured by travel documentarians THE 461 CULTURE / AIO Delights in their video "Araku coffee special: Our coffee, our pride 🌿", the story of Araku coffee is deeply intertwined with indigenous tribal livelihoods, sustainable agriculture, and serene mountain tourism.

The History of Coffee in Araku (1898 to Today)

Coffee cultivation in the Eastern Ghats began around 1898, when a British administrator named Broome planted the first coffee bushes along the Ananthagiri hills. While it started as a modest experiment, the high altitude, red loamy soil, and balanced monsoon moisture created ideal micro-climate conditions.

After Indian independence, the state's Girijan Cooperative Corporation (GCC) and later pioneering initiatives by the Naandi Foundation organized tens of thousands of tribal adivasi farmers into a global regenerative agriculture cooperative. Today, Araku Coffee has its own flagship luxury boutique store in Paris and boasts international organic certification.

What Makes Araku Coffee Taste So Distinctive?

Regenerative Shade Cultivation

  • Two-Tier Canopy Shade: Unlike sun-grown plantation mono-crops, Araku coffee bushes grow beneath towering silver oak, mango, and jackfruit trees. This slows down berry maturation, intensifying natural sugars and complex aromatic oils.
  • Intercropped with Black Pepper: Pepper vines coil around the shade trees right beside coffee plants, imparting subtle spicy undertones to the soil.
  • Zero Chemical Pesticides: Local adivasi communities use traditional composts, cattle manure, and bio-fertilizers.
  • Selective Hand-Picking: Only deep crimson ripe berries are harvested by hand, ensuring superior cup clarity without bitter unripe beans.

Best Months to Visit the Plantations

  • Blossom Season (April to May): Following early summer pre-monsoon showers, the entire valley blooms with delicate white jasmine-like coffee flowers. The air carries an intoxicating floral fragrance.
  • Harvest Season (November to February): The hills turn crimson as berries ripen. This is the best time for photography, watching pulp processing, and enjoying crisp winter weather.

Visiting the Araku Coffee Museum

Located conveniently in Araku town opposite the bus station, the Araku Coffee Museum is an absolute must-visit for coffee lovers:

Visitor Information

  • Timings: 9:00 AM to 8:00 PM (Open all 7 days)
  • Entry Fee: Nominal fee (~₹20 per person)
  • Exhibits: Historical dioramas tracing the origin of coffee in Ethiopia to the Eastern Ghats, antique roasting and grinding machines.
  • Tasting Counter: Sample hot freshly roasted espresso, cappuccino, or cold brews along with handcrafted coffee bean chocolates and pastries.

Where to Buy Authentic Araku Coffee

Buyer's Guide

1. GCC (Girijan Cooperative Corporation) Outlets: Government-certified pure organic coffee beans and powder at fair prices that directly benefit tribal farmers.

2. Coffee Museum Store: Offers specialty single-origin roasted beans, medium-dark roasts, and gift boxes.

3. Ananthagiri Hill Stalls: Roadside tribal stalls selling freshly ground filter coffee powders packaged straight from local village processing units.
