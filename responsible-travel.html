Ethical Framework

Responsible Travel Charter

Araku Valley is home to diverse Adivasi and indigenous farming communities. We operate with strict principles of dignity, ecological care, and economic fairness.

1. Direct Community Revenue

The majority of all trail and experience payments goes directly into the hands of community guides, homestay hosts, and village cooks. We believe tourism must be an economic catalyst owned by the valley's inhabitants, not by distant booking platforms.

2. Explicit Photography Consent

Always ask before taking photos. Never photograph residents, children, homes, or private rituals without clear verbal permission. Community members are hosts, professionals, and neighbors — not spectacle.

3. Group Size Caps

To prevent ecological degradation along delicate hill ridges and to avoid overwhelming small farming settlements, all community-guided treks are strictly capped at 8 to 10 participants.

4. No Plastic & Zero Waste

Single-use plastic bottles and polythene bags are strictly prohibited on trail paths. We provide clean filtered water in refillable containers, and all waste generated during trips is packed out completely.

5. Cultural Respect & Village Protocol

Alcohol and disruptive commercial behavior are strictly barred inside village hamlets. Please do not hand out money, sweets, or gifts directly to children, as this fosters dependency. If you wish to support local education, consult our guides about official village school funds.
